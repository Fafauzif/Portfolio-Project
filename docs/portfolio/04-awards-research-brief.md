# 04 — Awards: Research Brief & Page Specs

Five placings across four disciplines. Externally judged. Almost no other candidate in his cohort has five of these. Right now they are five naked bullets on LinkedIn, which means the rarest thing he owns is invisible.

**This file is the shopping list for the deep search.**

---

## Part 1 — Why awards deserve five pages

A list of awards says "things happened to me." An award page says "here is what I built, here is who judged it, here is why it won."

The gap between those two is the entire value. So each page must answer:

1. **What was the ask?** The competition's brief, in its own words.
2. **What were the constraints?** Word count, page limit, team size, time, format, round structure.
3. **What did he make?** The actual artifact, visible.
4. **What problem did it solve, or what argument did it make?** The substance.
5. **Why did it place?** Mapped to the *published judging criteria*. This is the block almost nobody writes and it's the block that proves he understands what good looks like.
6. **What did he learn / what would he change?**

---

## Part 2 — What to hunt for, per competition

For each of the five, collect in this priority order. **Highest value first.**

| Priority | Artifact | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Recording of the performance** (speech) / **the written submission** (essay) / **the deck** (business plan) | The primary evidence. Without it there's no page. |
| 2 | **The judges' brief or rubric** | Unlocks the "why it placed" block and proves strategic understanding |
| 3 | **Official result announcement / certificate** | Verifiable third-party proof — link to it |
| 4 | **Jury feedback or score sheets**, if released | Rare and extremely high value |
| 5 | **Entrant numbers** ("top 10 of 400") | Makes the placing legible — a national 2nd is different if 12 people entered |
| 6 | **Photos, program book, press coverage** | Texture, and shows it was real |
| 7 | **His own notes, drafts, working files** | Great for showing process; often the only thing that still exists |

### Where these usually live

- **Own Google Drive / old laptop folders** — check first, fastest
- **The competition's official Instagram** — result announcements, finalist lists, often still posted
- **The organiser's website or Linktree** — briefs and rubrics are sometimes still up
- **WhatsApp groups** from the committee — search the group name and "Guidebook", "Buku Panduan", "Rubrik Penilaian"
- **Email** — search for the competition name; committee emails carry the guidebook as an attachment
- **YouTube** — speech finals are frequently uploaded by organisers or by the university
- **National news / university press releases** — Indonesian campus news outlets cover business plan wins

### Naming convention for what he gathers


One folder per competition. If a folder has `submission` + `brief`, I can write a complete page. If it has only `result`, the page is a paragraph.

## Part 4 — Page template (shared by all five)

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  [Discipline]              [Year]                   │  mono, muted
│                                                     │
│  Competition name, large, serif                     │
│  [Placing] — set in the accent colour, not gold     │
│                                                     │
│  One sentence: what the competition asked for.      │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│  THE ASK      │  THE CONSTRAINTS  │  THE RESULT     │  3-col band
│  what was     │  words / pages /  │  placed 2nd of  │
│  required     │  rounds / team    │  N entrants     │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│  THE WORK — the artifact, full width                │
│  (deck gallery · essay text · speech video)         │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│  WHY IT PLACED — mapped to judging criteria,        │
│  one line of criteria + one line of how it scored   │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│  WHAT I'D CHANGE — two sentences                    │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│  Verify: link to official result · ← next award     │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Rules for the award pages

- **State the placing in the accent colour, never gold or bronze.** Metal-coloured medals read as a template. Type and colour hierarchy can carry the ranking with more authority than a graphic.
- **Never use laurel wreath icons.** It's the single loudest "stock template" signal in a student portfolio.
- **Always state entrant count if it's known.** "2nd place" is meaningless without scale; "2nd of 214 teams" is a credential.
- **Link to the official announcement.** Third-party verification is worth more than any layout.
- **Mono for the facts, serif for the argument.** Dates, placings, counts, organisers → mono. The one-sentence summary and the "why it placed" reasoning → serif. That split is the whole typographic system in miniature.

## Part 5 — What to send me

Message by message, or drop into `docs/portfolio/assets/awards/`:

1. The submitted file for each (PDF, doc, video link)
2. The guidebook or rubric if it still exists
3. The official result announcement — link or screenshot
4. Everything remembered about scale: how many entrants, how many rounds, who judged
5. The idea/argument itself, in a couple of sentences, for anything where the artifact is lost

**If an artifact is permanently gone**, the page still works — it just becomes text-forward, built from memory and the official announcement. Say so plainly on the page ("the original submission is no longer available; this is a reconstruction") rather than quietly implying the artifact exists. Honesty here is cheap and buys enormous credibility.
