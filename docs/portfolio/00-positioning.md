## 1. What this site is for

## 3. The positioning

> **Marketing strategist who closes the loop: research → strategy → execution → a number that proves it. And when the tool doesn't exist, he builds it.**

Short forms:

- **On the site hero:** *"I don't stop at the deck."*
- **In one line:** *Strategy that ships, with the receipts.*
- **The category he claims:** Marketing & Communications Strategist — **not** "content writer", **not** "social media specialist", **not** "creative".

### Why that category and not the obvious ones

Every marketing student in Indonesia can claim "social media", "content", "copywriting". Those categories are crowded, commoditised, and price-anchored low.

Three things Fauzi has that almost nobody in his cohort has *together*:

1. **A measured cause-and-effect chain.** He doesn't say "we grew engagement." He says median ER was ~154% above historical benchmarks and 62.2% of views came from non-followers. That's analyst language, and it's rare.
2. **A working system he built himself.** The Apps Script referral engine is genuinely unusual. A marketing hire who can ship internal tooling is a different job grade.
3. **Proof he can stand up and be judged.** Two national business-plan placings, a national essay placing, a national speech placing, an international speech final. That's four different formats of being assessed by strangers and placing each time. It says: coherent under pressure.

### The throughline to state loudly, once, near the top

**Research → Insight → Strategy → System → Measurement.**

Every project on the site is the same shape in different clothes. TEDxUI: strategy doc → 17 posts → benchmarked metrics. Gandeng: audience insight → 14 posts → 691 applicants, and then a tool to scale the next intake. MavenPRO: 40-respondent survey + 2 interviews + 3-competitor benchmark → 20-slide deck the client said was accurate.

That repetition isn't a weakness to hide. It's the product. Say it once, then let each case study prove it again.

## 4. The honesty constraint (non-negotiable)

He is a third-year student. TEDxUI Director of Marketing is a student-organisation role. **Never dress it up as a company job.** A sharp recruiter spots inflation instantly and it costs the whole site's credibility.

The rule: **label the organisation plainly, then let the work speak at professional scale.** "Director of Marketing, TEDxUniversitasIndonesia — a student-run organisation. 557,080 views in 30 days." 557k views is 557k views regardless of who paid for it. The framing does the work, not the title inflation.

This also makes the site *more* impressive: a 21-year-old who benchmarked his own org's performance against its historical baseline is doing something most working professionals don't bother to do.

## 5. Goals

1. Position him in the **strategist** category, not the content category.
2. Make all five competition placings **visible and legible**, with the artifact attached — these are his rarest assets and they are currently naked bullets.
3. Show the **referral engine** as a first-class project, not a bullet under a job.
4. Give a recruiter a **90-second path** and a **10-minute path** to the same conclusion.
5. Be a piece of craft that would survive on a design feed — because that *is* the portfolio for a communications role.
6. Convert: CV download, email, LinkedIn, and a plain "here's how to reach me."

## 6. Non-goals

- Not a blog. Writing exists to prove a capability, not to build an audience yet.
- Not a CMS. Content changes maybe monthly; hardcoded data files are fine.
- Not a design-tool showcase. No animation that a recruiter has to wait for.
- Not a résumé in HTML. Structure by **outcome**, not chronology.

## 7. Anti-goals — what would make this a failure even if it technically works

- **Slow on a mid-range Android on 4G.** The primary audience is Indonesian recruiters on a phone. A site that takes 6 seconds is a site nobody sees. This is the single biggest execution risk of the "3D wizardry" ambition.
- **Style over substance.** If the 3D is the memorable part, the site failed. The numbers must be the memorable part; the 3D should only be the thing that makes them *feel* consequential.
- **AI-slop tells.** Purple gradient, Inter everywhere, glassmorphism cards, generic "passionate about" copy, a fake dashboard screenshot, emoji bullets, three-column icon features. Any one of these reads as "made in an afternoon."
- **Unverifiable claims.** Every number must trace to something he can produce if asked in an interview. No rounded-up growth percentages.
- **Illegible on a phone.** Non-negotiable: the mobile layout is designed first, not adapted last.
- **Fails accessibility.** A flex piece that a screen reader can't navigate isn't a flex, it's a liability. Target WCAG 2.2 AA.
