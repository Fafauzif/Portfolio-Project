## Unlearning the word portfolio

The workshop was open to beginners, but the application put portfolio examples near the top. Prospective participants told admissions staff that they might return after making more work.

I sat in on intake calls, read the questions applicants sent by email, and compared how the invite differed from the application. The mismatch came down to one word. The school meant a sample of curiosity. Applicants heard a showreel.

## Change the thing people apply with

We tried writing a clearer FAQ and suggesting alternative files. The stronger change was to ask for a photograph of one thing they wanted to change, together with a short explanation. The admissions team could learn how someone looked at a problem without asking them to know design conventions.

I wrote the invitation and form prompts. Admissions staff tested the review criteria and we selected actual prompts from the workshop experience to use in the outreach material.

## What the team kept

We delivered an intake page, an accessible poster set, and sample responses for the admissions desk. The team kept the sample-photo prompt for the next workshop and saved the questions people still asked for the next copy review. The review confirmed that the prompt was usable. Assessing recruitment equity would require a separate look at who heard about the workshop and who could attend.

At the same school, I tried a related exercise inside the workshop itself. [A Room for Questions](/work/a-room-for-questions/) gives participants time to put what they are unsure about into words before someone takes the floor.
