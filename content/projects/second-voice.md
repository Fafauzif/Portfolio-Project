## Listen before writing the connective lines

I joined after the interviews were recorded. Early pages asked the narrator to repeat a detail immediately before an interviewee explained it. The listener had to hear the same idea twice to reach what mattered.

I marked places where a voice carried a sensory observation, where the chronology needed help, and where a pause gave the scene room to breathe.

## Give each voice a job

The narrator locates the listener in time and place. The interviewee carries their own experience. I cut duplicate lines and added brief transitions where it was unclear who we were about to hear. I also flagged a metaphor that sounded elegant on the page but was hard to parse by ear.

The producer kept editorial ownership of the episode. I prepared the revised script and reading notes for the recording session.

## A spoken check

In a table read, we listened for names that needed repeating and lines that ran longer than a comfortable breath. The revised script went forward to a production draft. The editing record retains the distinction between supplied interview material and added narration.
