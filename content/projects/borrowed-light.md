## An empty room with many rules

The library extended its hours but still had spare study seats in the evening. Staff knew some regulars were coming later. They wanted to understand why the space felt inaccessible to people in nearby classes and workplaces.

I interviewed patrons and observed the switch from staffed to self-serve hours. People who arrived after dinner needed confidence that their card worked, that there was somewhere to sit, and that showing up would not disrupt the room.

## Write a visit you can picture

Working with front-desk staff, I scored three interventions against cost and ease of upkeep. A booking tool offered predictability at the cost of adding a new platform. We developed a small “first evening” guide and a visible handover notice at the front door. The guide explains the ordinary visit: tap in, choose a seat, find a power socket, leave when you need to.

A member of the library team drew the floor plan. I worked on the access language, revised the notice after a walkthrough, and created the brief for a first-use test.

## Watch where people hesitate

We handed the library the guide, sign, and an observation sheet. The suggested signals were abandoned entries, repeated help requests, and empty-seat periods. Higher traffic was a possible outcome. Understanding where patrons hesitated gave the library a clearer first measure.
