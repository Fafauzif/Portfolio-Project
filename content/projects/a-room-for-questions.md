## The late question

A workshop agenda often puts questions after the last slide. By that point, participants have started to pack up, and the first person to speak can determine the rest of the conversation. I wanted a format that gives the room time to make questions before anyone asks one aloud.

## Change the order of the session

I designed a short talk interrupted by two periods of quiet writing. Participants could then exchange a question or keep it to themselves. Each prompt asks for a specific point of uncertainty, such as a term that needs an example. The group starts the discussion from the written questions. That helps me get an idea of what the room needs before choosing the examples to include next.

## Material the group took away

I facilitated the session and left the host with a cue sheet, a prompt set, and a shortened version for a shorter class block. The prompt that worked best asked which term needed an example. It regularly surfaced a practical gap the speaker thought they had already covered.
