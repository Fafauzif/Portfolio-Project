## A reading exercise in the opening credits

I wanted to write about title sequences without summarizing film plots. The zine asks what the viewer learns from pace, alignment, and where a name appears. Each spread takes one behavior and reconstructs it with invented text.

## From video to paper

A frame-by-frame account can be technically clear and impossible to read in print. I used short runs of different widths to show how long an image stays on screen. On a spread about type that arrives late, the names appear as the reader reaches the next fold in the page.

The decision was to treat paper as its own medium. A strip of related views gives the reader room to pause and examine a transition the original sequence moves through quickly.

## What went into the edition

I wrote the essays, developed the layout, and printed a short first edition to test reading order and paper weight. A second run would need a quieter running caption. I made it too easy to jump ahead and read the conclusion of a spread before encountering the sequence.
