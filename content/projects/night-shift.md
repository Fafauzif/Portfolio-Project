## Start with the journey home

Afterimage had assembled a late program of short talks and gallery tours. The early brief treated evening as a visual theme. I reframed it as a practical window. People needed to know if they could come straight from work, what stayed open, and how late they could arrive.

I mapped those questions from the entrance, the transit stop, and the booking page. Visitor services contributed the answers and our designer developed the nighttime visual language.

## Design one useful second look

I wrote a headline that named the hours, followed by a short guide to what could fit into the evening. Existing visitors saw a familiar museum visit with an unfamiliar schedule. People finding it on a street poster could take in the essential facts before the light changed.

The team kept edition-specific artwork, but set times and access details in a fixed location on every piece. I prepared the writing guide and tested copy against conversation prompts with the front-of-house team.

## After the first Friday

The installed posters and booking page exposed a mismatch between advertised arrival and the last admission window. We revised the booking confirmation and added the later-show entry rules to the street material. I handed over a small set of timed copy templates for future evenings. The project left the museum with a reusable communications rhythm and a better handoff between marketing and the people at the door.
