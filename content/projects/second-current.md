## The competition brief

A regional event network asked for a viable way to keep temporary signage in circulation. We worked as a three-person team. I owned the field research and service concept, helped define the pilot, and presented the operational trade-offs. A teammate built the cost model and another designed the physical examples.

## Observe the end of the event

Early ideas centered on recyclable materials and a searchable stock library. After talking with venue staff and walking through a teardown, we focused on the last hour of an event. Teams had little time to sort mixed sizes, no obvious place to store them, and no reason to hold an unlabeled object for someone they had never met.

That pointed to a service problem. A venue could buy a better sign and still discard it if there was no predictable collection route.

## Standardize what goes back

We proposed interchangeable frames with replaceable faces, a check-out sheet, and return crates that stack the same way regardless of venue. A collection partner would inspect returned parts and send usable pieces to the next booking. Scratches could be logged at return without taking an entire frame out of service.

Instead of launching a public marketplace, we designed a closed pilot around two hosts with recurring bookings. The small network let us follow one object through several handovers and made transport routes more predictable.

## Set the boundary of the model

Our model compared rental, repair, cleaning, collection, and loss across several reuse cycles. The scenario depended on a stable return rate and short transport routes. We recommended recording condition and staff time before estimating any cost or material saving. A missing crate, for instance, changes both the collection cost and whether the next host can fulfill a booking.

## The pitch and the next test

We used a single sign to explain the whole system during the pitch, including the seam where the face detaches. Jurors asked who pays when a frame is damaged. That question exposed a missing policy, and we revised the proposal to put inspection and damage rules in the rental terms.

The concept received a jury commendation. The team delivered a service blueprint, sample frame, and scenario worksheet. The proposed pilot would start with owned inventory and a short return window before testing an inter-venue exchange.
