## The decision before the application

Joining a mentorship program takes more effort than liking an announcement. The recruitment campaign had to give prospective mentees a clearer picture of the program, help them decide whether it fit their plans, and make the route to applying easy to find.

I led program branding for G-Consulting at Gandeng Foundation. I directed campaign planning, copy, distribution, and reporting with two associates and helped coordinate outside amplification.

## Give every post a job

Our tracked recruitment work ran from 22 June to 21 July 2026. We used the posts to do different jobs: introduce the opportunity, explain it, build familiarity, and point readers to the application. The campaign tracker made the row-level performance easier to compare without losing the context of what each post was for.

One of my assigned posts was “Stay Tuned.” It served as an early invitation to pay attention, before the campaign moved into application information. The work on the feed was part of a wider mix that also included partner distribution and direct routes to the form.

## Keep the application path visible

I also built the [Gandeng Referral Workflow](/work/gandeng-referral-workflow/), a separate Google Apps Script tool for handling referrals. The campaign brought people to the program. The workflow addressed the smaller operational problem of issuing and checking a referral without adding another manual list.

## What the tracker showed

The 14 tracked recruitment posts recorded 357,491 impressions and 11,116 engagements. The program closed with 691 applicants. In their application responses, 477 cited one of Gandeng’s two Instagram accounts as a discovery source. That distinction mattered for the campaign review: the post metrics describe activity on the platform, while the source responses describe where applicants remember first finding the opportunity.
