## Price was only part of the brief

The case brief gave our team a rehearsal-space network and its booking histories. We first considered different prices by time of day. The histories exposed a more consequential uncertainty. Staff treated a tentative request and a repeat booking as if both carried the same commitment.

My teammates studied acquisition and operations. I worked on booking scenarios, synthesized the decision criteria, and wrote the comparison slide.

## Model the booking before modeling the growth

We proposed shared standard session blocks, a repeat-booking option, and a clear expiry on reserved time. That design made the capacity plan easier to update. We modeled different rates of take-up and cancellation before attaching any forecast.

Under low repeat-booking uptake, the blocks created less benefit and sometimes reduced flexibility. We made that trade-off visible in the model and suggested testing one location during a season with steadier demand.

## What the jury heard

We submitted a capacity worksheet, a booking-policy concept, and a short pitch. The project was a finalist. The model represents a scenario for the case brief. It identifies the first question to test: how much demand would shift to repeat bookings?
