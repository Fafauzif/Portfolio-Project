## Set expectations early

The cooperative wanted to offer gentle lighting, lower sound, and flexible movement during selected shows. The first announcement celebrated the idea but left visitors to infer what would change in the room.

I wrote the audience-facing brief after speaking to the projectionist and front-of-house staff. We reviewed the experience from buying a ticket to deciding whether to step outside during a show.

## Pair the promise with practical details

We turned the campaign into a two-layer invitation. The poster names the screening and makes the offer easy to remember. The event page gives the exact differences: the lighting level, movement policy, and quiet area location. The main web page gives those details in selectable, readable text.

My work covered naming, copy, information layout, and a checklist the team could use when programming future screenings.

## The routine behind the announcement

The operators put the same checklist on their show handoff. That helped a new staff member explain what would happen without searching a previous social post. Front-of-house notes recorded anonymous questions and practical changes for the next session.
