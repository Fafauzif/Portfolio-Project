## A sentence at the edge of a note

I started with a sticky note left behind in a meeting room. It contained one sentence and no context. The team remembered what it meant on the day. A week later we had turned it into a bullet that made a much stronger claim than the original conversation.

## Write from a working example

The first draft was a broad defense of keeping research messy. I cut the argument back and followed one hypothetical note through a practical workflow: retain the observation, write the interpretation beside it, and add a decision only when the team actually makes it. The same note can support a decision, contradict it, or stay unresolved.

I drafted and revised the essay with an editor who asked for an example that a small team could reproduce without buying a tool.

## From the essay

> A note can travel without pretending to be a conclusion. Leave enough of the conversation attached for the next person to disagree with you.

The finished essay includes a printable page for trying the exercise. It was the starting point for some of the navigation decisions in [Source Atlas](/work/source-atlas/).
