## Change one thing at a time

A series of early renders looked convincing individually and unrelated together. I wanted the object to stay the same while the surface changed from glazed to porous. Moving the light to flatter one texture would hide the comparison.

I fixed the camera and built a rounded form with enough curve to carry a highlight. Three versions share the same model. The difference comes from roughness, edge wear, and the size of the surface variation.

## Keep a material notebook

I saved a small crop of the rim and the shadow with each iteration. These close views revealed problems more clearly than the most dramatic wide shots. A rough surface became readable once I reduced the scale of the pores and allowed some variation in specular response.

I used a Blender assistant to name nodes and compare light setups. I chose the final form and tuned the surface by inspecting how the rim caught the light.

## What the comparison made visible

The images form a comparison sheet as well as a small gallery. A single set of rendering conditions gives each change meaning. If I take this into animation, I will retain the static comparison and add a restrained turntable as a second way to examine the object.
