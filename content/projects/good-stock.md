## Find the assumption inside the request

A community fridge can have room in the morning and nowhere to put a delivery by the time a café closes. Our team built a small prototype centered on available capacity, shelf life, and collection slots. I owned the interaction flow and wrote the request and confirmation states.

## Make a narrow match

We considered map-first browsing, a live chat, and a claim queue. A queue worked best with the information we could verify: an available collection window, a rough quantity, and a receiving organization with capacity. The prototype distinguishes “someone is interested” from “someone is coming.”

For a possible live trial, we identified checks requiring a receiving coordinator, including food eligibility and storage conditions. We used synthetic records in the demo.

## Demo and follow-up

The team showed a handoff and simulated a missed collection. Reassigning a slot proved more helpful than adding another public notification. We completed a clickable demo and an operations map. A later release would need policies and onboarding owned by the participating organizations.
